JackMoebius

Expose native macOS apps (Safari, Music, FaceTime…) as JACK clients with JackMoebius: route any CoreAudio app in and out of the JACK graph from JackMate.

JackMoebius and JackMate, made to work together.

★ New in 2.0.0

JackMoebius lets JackMate expose any native macOS application (Safari, Music, FaceTime, your DAW’s companion apps) as a Jack client. Apps that have no built-in Jack support can then send and receive audio through the Jack graph like any other client, and interact with your native Jack applications.

Important

JackMoebius is a separate virtual audio driver. JackMate drives it, but the daemon must be installed on your Mac for any of this to work (see Installing JackMoebius).

What is JackMoebius

JackMoebius is a bidirectional CoreAudio ↔︎ Jack bridge: the next-generation, functional successor to JackRouter (abandoned since 2020).

Most macOS apps speak only CoreAudio; they know nothing about Jack. JackMoebius creates, on demand, a Jack client for each app you choose to expose, in both directions:

  • Output: the app’s sound is injected into the Jack graph (route Safari into Ardour, record a FaceTime call, capture a game…).
  • Input: a capture-capable app receives audio from the Jack graph (feed a processed signal back into it).

Everything is driven from JackMate: you never edit a config file or touch the daemon directly.

Installing JackMoebius

JackMoebius is a separate application: a paid app with a 14-day free trial. Get it from its page and install it on your Mac; JackMate detects the installed daemon automatically, with no path to configure.

Note

Once installed, the JackMoebius version appears in the Configuration header, next to Jack’s (see Version indicator). Keep it up to date: JackMate signals when a newer version is available.

Prefer to see it first? A short tour where you expose your macOS apps to JACK, wire and mix them in JackMate, and save the whole setup as a studio:

Enabling exposure

Turn JackMoebius on from the Configuration view, with the “Expose native macOS apps to JACK” option.

  • When enabled, JackMoebius starts automatically right after Jack. It does not change the jackd command in any way.
  • A dedicated button in the patchbay toolbar opens the JackMoebius panel (below).
  • A JackMoebius status indicator sits next to Jack’s in the sidebar, so you always see whether the daemon is up.
Tip

The daemon’s live status is event-driven: JackMate reflects a start, stop or crash immediately, and automatically restarts JackMoebius if it goes down while Jack is running.

The JackMoebius panel

The slide-in panel lists every running CoreAudio app and lets you expose it to the graph. Each row shows the app’s real icon and name, with:

Control Action
Output switch Expose the app’s sound to the graph
Input switch Give the app an input from the graph (greyed out for apps that can’t capture)
Channels stepper Number of input channels (appears when Input is on)

Exposing an app makes its box appear in the patchbay instantly; turning it off removes the box.

A “system monitor” (master) box exposes the global system mix (everything macOS is playing) as a single Jack client, independent of the per-app selection.

Note

The daemon only sees an app once it enters the audio context (produces or captures sound). An app that is running but silent may take a moment to appear in the list.

Advanced configuration

An “eye-off” button removes an app from the selector (with a confirmation if it’s currently exposed), useful for apps you never want to route. The Advanced JackMoebius configuration panel (tabbed) lets you:

  • re-enable apps you previously excluded (blacklist);
  • free dormant channel reservations left by apps that have since quit.

In the patchbay

An exposed app appears as a regular node on the canvas, with two differences from ordinary Jack clients:

  • its badge is the real application icon (instead of the two-letter initials badge);
  • the master box carries the JackMoebius glyph.

Clicking the badge opens the usual info sheet, which identifies the box as a JackMoebius client.

Routing status

Because a native app routes audio through a specific CoreAudio device, JackMate watches that an exposed, wired app is actually using the right device:

Marker Meaning
⚠ amber on a box The app is exposed and wired, but isn’t using the correct audio device, so audio won’t flow
Discreet cue The app is selected but currently silent

An alert panel (opened automatically, or by clicking the ⚠) lists the affected apps and what to do. The fix is made in the app itself, or in macOS System Settings ▸ Sound, not in JackMate (JackMate can’t force another app’s device).

Per-app volumes

In the JackMoebius panel, every exposed app has a volume control. By default it is locked (padlock closed): the app follows the “JackMoebius Out” master level. Click the padlock to unlock it (padlock open, cyan) and set an independent volume for that app with the slider.

Padlock Behaviour
🔒 locked The app follows the master volume (default)
🔓 unlocked The app has its own independent volume
Tip

The master output volumes (Jack’s output device and “JackMoebius Out”) are set from the Volumes bar at the top of the patchbay. See Output volumes.

Version indicator

The Configuration header shows the installed JackMoebius version next to Jack’s. As with Jack, it signals when a newer version is available so you can keep the daemon current.

In studios

A studio saves and restores your complete JackMoebius state: which apps are exposed (Output / Input and their channel counts), the master monitor, and per-app volumes, along with the output-device volumes. Load a studio and JackMate restarts the daemon if needed, relaunches and re-exposes the apps, and restores every volume. See Studios for the details.
